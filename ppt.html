Where it breaks down

  • Dense digital forms. Pain scales and dropdowns feel like paperwork handed to a patient.
  • Type that cannot be read. Presbyopia and tremor are the default here, not the edge case.
  • Tech anxiety. Fear of pressing the wrong thing ends the session before it starts.
  • Isolation. Exercising alone at 7am carries no social cost for skipping it.

Two people, one interaction

The user: Indian adults 60+

On a structured mobility plan. Comfortable with WhatsApp and voice notes, not with app stores.

The buyer: their adult children

Often in another city. Pays for the programme and wants proof it is being used.

The person who uses this is not the person who buys it. One daily interaction has to satisfy both.

A companion, not a form

Solution & avatar thesis

Asha is a lightweight, voice-first progressive web app. It opens from a link, greets the member by name, and takes under three minutes end to end.

  1. 1

    Sign in, once

    Supabase auth keeps each member's history attached to them, so day two knows what day one said.

  2. 2

    Three-step check-in, spoken

    Where it hurts, how much, how the energy feels. Answered by talking, not tapping.

  3. 3

    Two-minute seated routine

    Mobility and breathing, voice-paced and adjusted to the answers just given.

Why an avatar, and not a better form

A clinical checklist asks

"Rate your pain on a scale of 0 to 10."

Asha asks

"आपके घुटने आज कैसे लग रहे हैं?"

Both capture the same number. Only one still gets answered on day nine. Seniors resist reporting to a system and will readily report to a person, so Asha takes the posture of a person and keeps the structured data the care team needs.

Installed to the home screen straight from the browser. It sits next to WhatsApp and behaves like any other app, with no store account and no download step for the senior.

Four decisions, and who they were made for

Product & architecture

Each technical call was settled by one question: what does this change for a 68-year-old at 7am?

Speed treated as a feature

Groq API running openai/gpt-oss-20b at roughly 1,000 tokens per second.

A voice assistant that pauses is read as broken, and a senior does not try a second time. Sub-second replies are what make it feel like a conversation instead of a loading screen.

Accessibility as the base case

56px touch targets, WCAG AAA contrast, and live switching between English, हिंदी and Hinglish.

Most Indian seniors think in one language and read interfaces in another. Switching mid-session removed the most common reason a tester stopped and handed the phone back.

Zero download friction

A progressive web app in the browser, installable to the home screen.

No store account, no password reset, no update prompt. The link is the product, which also makes it forwardable by the family member already helping.

Data that leaves the app usefully

Supabase database and auth for profile history, plus a one-tap WhatsApp caregiver report.

Continuity for the physiotherapist, visibility for the daughter paying for the programme, delivered on the channel families already use.

Distribution through the caregiver

Go-to-market & validation

Seniors do not download apps. The people who help them with their phones do, and those people are already in a WhatsApp group about their parents' health.

Channel

The MVP went into family WhatsApp groups across Bihar and Odisha and reached seniors through the person who already helps them. Acquisition cost is zero, first-run support is solved by someone in the room, and the caregiver is inside the loop before the product asks them to be.

Method

  • Moderated morning check-ins, watching where a senior hesitates rather than asking afterwards.
  • Unmoderated sessions from the same cohort, to see what survives without a helper present.
  • Testers aged 58 to 74, on the phone they already own.
!

Safety guardrail

A reported pain level of 8 or above halts the routine immediately. Asha stops prompting exercises, tells the member to rest, and flags the session for the care team rather than continuing.

A wellness companion that keeps coaching through severe pain is a liability. This rule was written before the first tester opened the app.

What I was actually testing

Not whether people liked it. Whether a senior could complete a check-in and a routine unassisted, in their own language, on their own phone, on the first attempt.

What this proves, and what it does not. Eight sessions in one morning shows the interaction works unassisted and that voice is the preferred input. It says nothing yet about retention. Seven-day adherence is the metric I would hold this product to, and it needs a fortnight to read.

What the first users changed my mind about

Learnings & roadmap

Hindi voice support was the single feature that kept people going, but the app moved faster than their bodies did. Seniors need three to five extra seconds of silence between exercise transitions to reposition safely. Pacing is not a polish item here. It is the difference between a routine that gets finished and one abandoned halfway.

If I had two more weeks

Week 1

Care-team portal

Pipe pain and adherence data through a webhook into the Kinetic Age physiotherapist dashboard, so a member trending the wrong way is flagged before the next review instead of after it. This is the step that turns Asha from a standalone app into part of the programme.

Success: a physiotherapist changes one plan because of something Asha surfaced.

Week 2

Native WhatsApp and streaks

Move the check-in into a WhatsApp bot so the senior never leaves the app they already trust, and add mobility consistency streaks framed for this audience: gentle, non-competitive, rewarding showing up rather than performance.

Success: seven-day adherence above 60% without a family reminder.
